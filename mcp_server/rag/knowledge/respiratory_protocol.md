# Respiratory Deterioration Recognition & Escalation Protocol
**Source:** Respiratory Critical Care Committee | Version 3.1 | 2024-03

## 1. Scope

Applies to all inpatient areas. Identifies patients at risk of acute respiratory failure
requiring early intervention before cardiorespiratory arrest.

## 2. Respiratory Assessment — Primary Survey

**A — Airway**
- Patient speaking in full sentences = patent airway
- Stridor, gurgling, absent phonation = airway compromise → immediate review

**B — Breathing**
- Respiratory rate (count for full 60 seconds)
- SpO2 (continuous if NEWS2 ≥3)
- Use of accessory muscles, paradoxical breathing, cyanosis
- Auscultation: bilateral air entry, wheeze, crackles

**C — Circulation**
- HR, BP, capillary refill
- Cyanosis (central/peripheral)

## 3. SpO2 Target Ranges

| Patient Type | SpO2 Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard adult | 94–98% | Default for most patients |
| COPD/hypercapnic risk | 88–92% | Avoid O2-driven hypoventilation |
| Post-cardiac arrest | 94–98% | Avoid hyperoxia |
| Carbon monoxide poisoning | 100% | High-flow O2 until carboxyHb <5% |
| Neonates | 91–95% | Risk of retinopathy with higher values |

## 4. NEWS2 Respiratory Parameters

**Respiratory Rate scoring:**
- 12–20/min = 0 (normal)
- 9–11/min = 1 (mildly low)
- 21–24/min = 2 (elevated)
- ≤8/min or ≥25/min = 3 (critical)

**SpO2 scoring (Scale 1 — no hypercapnic risk):**
- ≥96% = 0
- 94–95% = 1
- 92–93% = 2
- ≤91% = 3

## 5. Oxygen Delivery Devices

| Device | FiO2 Range | Flow (L/min) | Indication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nasal cannula | 24–44% | 1–6 | Mild hypoxia, SpO2 92–95% |
| Simple face mask | 35–50% | 5–10 | Moderate hypoxia |
| Non-rebreather mask | 60–80% | 10–15 | Acute severe hypoxia |
| Venturi mask | 24–60% | Variable | COPD, precise FiO2 needed |
| CPAP (10cmH2O) | 40–100% | High flow | Cardiogenic pulmonary oedema, OSA |
| NIV/BiPAP | Variable | High flow | COPD exacerbation, hypercapnic failure |
| Intubation/mechanical ventilation | 21–100% | Ventilator | Refractory hypoxaemia, apnoea |

## 6. Escalation Thresholds — Respiratory

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| SpO2 <94% on room air | Commence supplemental O2, inform RN |
| SpO2 <90% on O2 | Urgent medical review within 15 min |
| SpO2 <85% | Emergency review — call MET/RRT now |
| RR ≥25/min | Senior review within 30 min |
| RR ≥30/min | Emergency review within 10 min |
| Use of accessory muscles + SpO2 <93% | Consider NIV/HDU |
| Paradoxical breathing | Immediate airway support |
| Stridor | EMERGENCY — Anaesthetics now |
| Cyanosis | Emergency 2222 call |

## 7. Post-Extubation Protocol

High-risk patients (prolonged intubation, difficult airway, obesity, COPD):
- Monitor SpO2 continuously for minimum 4 hours post-extubation
- High-flow nasal O2 (HFNO) for first 2 hours (flow 40–60 L/min, FiO2 0.4–0.6)
- RR monitoring every 15 minutes first hour, then 30 min for 2 hours
- NEWS2 reassessment every hour for 4 hours
- If SpO2 drops <94% or RR >22, inform ICU registrar immediately

## 8. ARDS/Acute Respiratory Failure — Ventilator Strategy

Lung-protective ventilation (ARDSNet protocol):
- Tidal volume: 6 mL/kg predicted body weight
- Plateau pressure ≤30 cmH2O
- PEEP per ARDS Net low-PEEP table
- FiO2 titrated to SpO2 88–95%
- Prone positioning for PaO2/FiO2 <150 (consider)

## 9. Deterioration Trajectory Recognition

**Early warning pattern (act immediately):**
SpO2 trending down + RR trending up + HR compensatory rise
= classic pre-failure pattern; patient likely to deteriorate further

**Time-critical window:**
SpO2 95% → 90% → 85% typically occurs over minutes to hours
Identify the trend early; do not wait for crisis threshold

**Artefact vs. true desaturation:**
- Check probe placement (warm, well-perfused digit, correct orientation)
- Compare waveform quality (good = regular pulsatile, poor = flat/noisy)
- If perfusion index <0.3, relocate probe
- Check bilateral SpO2 if unilateral reading low
- Clinical correlation (cyanosis, distress, RR, consciousness)

## 10. Documentation Requirements

After any respiratory event:
- Record time of first deterioration, first O2 intervention, escalation call, response
- News2 score at time of event
- O2 device and flow rate used
- SpO2 before/during/after O2 therapy
- Clinical response (improved / no change / further deterioration)
